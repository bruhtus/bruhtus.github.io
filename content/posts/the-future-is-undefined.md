If you ever learn about programming, especially using C programming language,
you will most likely know about [undefined
behavior](https://en.cppreference.com/c/language/behavior). Undefined behavior
in C programming language is basically _anything can happen at that point_,
there are no restrictions on the behavior of the program. When we enter the
_undefined behavior_ zone, the program behavior become unpredictable, the
program can crash or even delete the root filesystem, anything is valid.

A lot of people didn't like _undefined behavior_ in C programming language
most likely because it's unpredictable or non-deterministic, but the benefit
of _undefined behavior_ is that we make the C programming language
implementation _very flexible_. Whether that is good or bad depends on your
perspective.

And then one day, I read about [this imaginary dialog between Oscar Wilde and
Artificial Intelligence
(AI)](https://jaysamit.substack.com/p/oscar-wilde-just-invented-the-perfect).
One thing that stand out to me from those dialog is "to define is to limit."
And that got me thinking, maybe we don't need to _define everything_, like the
future for example. The only thing that is certain in the future of all living
being is dead, and maybe that's because dead is the part of living being
_definition_ or _limitation_ (?). Other than that, I don't think anything is
certain in the future.

Some people try _to define_ the future, like "this is the future" or "that is
the future," and I am like "how did they know what would happen in the
future?" And I have a hypothesis that these people trying _to limit_ the
future to something that they already know or familiar with. Some people is
afraid of the unknown, so they are trying to make everything _known_,
including the future. Whether their claim will become true or not, that
remains to be seen. But the interesting part is that, by _defining the
future_, there is a possibility that someone will prevent that kind of future
to happen. We are not living in the future, we are living in the present, and
there's enough time to change what the future looks like. Maybe there's some
kind of paradox by _defining_ what would happen in the future, and what I mean
by that is by defining or claiming something would happen in the future, the
possibility of that future to happen can reduce depending on how many people
support the idea or not.

With that in mind, if you heard or read someone _claiming_ about the future,
you should take a skeptical stance to their statement. Do not limit your
future by someone else's definition. The future is undefined, anything
can happen in the future.
